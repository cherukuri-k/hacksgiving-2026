Aboutwhat we built

ComForce runs eight divisions on volunteers' phones. Relay gives them one number, one board, one proof.

One numberJordan answers in English or Spanish and logs the request while the caller is still talking. The resident gets a tracking code by text.
One boardEvery division sees the same card. One person claims it, a partner is dispatched, a volunteer gets a run sheet. Nobody chases the same family twice.
One proofAfter delivery Relay asks the resident: did it arrive? Their answer — not the helper's — closes the case and lands in the funder report.

01 / 05Information sharing & coordination

Today every line in this picture is a phone. Relay puts one record in the middle.

The problem

Eight divisions and a dozen partners coordinate by text, Facebook and memory. Who has beds tonight? Who is already on the 35th Street family? Nobody can see the whole picture.

How Relay solves it

A shared partner network with live capacity, and one tap to dispatch: Jordan calls the partner, records the answer, and texts the volunteer a run sheet.

02 / 05Community requests & referrals

A request moves through five states. Only the resident can move it to the last one.

The problem

Requests arrive on whichever phone is awake. They get handled or forgotten; nobody knows which. "Done" means the helper said so.

How Relay solves it

Phone, text or web becomes one card in under a minute. Claim-to-own, dispatch, deliver — then Relay texts the resident, or Jordan calls back in their language: did it arrive?

03 / 05Data collection & impact measurement synthetic 2026 data

The same numbers the City reports — plus the one it can't.

The problem

Impact is reconstructed at grant time from memory and screenshots. The City's follow-up figure is self-reported by the responder; the resident is never asked.

How Relay solves it

Every request logs itself. Outcomes are verified by residents and mapped to the City's 26 Countdown clusters. One click drafts the grant application in the funder's own vocabulary.

04 / 05Community awareness & decision support

Where is help asked for — and where isn't it arriving?

The problem

Leaders decide where to send people from anecdote. By the time a pattern is obvious, it's a month old.

How Relay solves it

Need vs. delivery by neighbourhood, refreshed as requests close. Coral marks clusters where requests outrun help. A weekly brief writes itself for the team meeting.

05 / 05Training & knowledge sharing

Volunteers come and go. The playbook shouldn't leave with them.

The problem

How to handle an eviction notice, who to call at 2 AM, what not to promise — it lives in the heads of people who burn out in eighteen months.

How Relay solves it

ComForce's playbooks are written once. "Ask the Guide" answers only from them, with the source. Every response gets a room, so the next shift reads the thread instead of starting over.
